# Acoustic Guitar Wood Types

## Summary
The most commonly used wood species for acoustic guitar soundboards are spruce, specifically Sitka spruce, which provides a bright and balanced tone. Mahogany and Koa are also used for soundboards, offering a warm and woody tone with an emphasized midrange. River Oak is a hardwood species commonly used for the back and sides of acoustic guitars, known for its stability and resistance to humidity changes. Ebony is a standard material for acoustic guitar necks and fingerboards. The choice of wood can influence the tone, volume, responsiveness, and projection of an acoustic guitar.

## Soundboard Wood Species
Spruce is considered a popular tonewood for acoustic guitar tops, with Sitka spruce being the most common type used in soundboard construction. [S1] [S2]

## Back and Sides Wood Species
River Oak is a hardwood species native to Australia, commonly used for the back and sides of acoustic guitars. [S5] [S6]

## Neck and Fingerboard Wood Materials
Ebony wood is a standard material for acoustic guitar necks and fingerboards. [S14]

## Limitations
The evidence does not establish a clear distinction between the wood choices for steel-string and classical acoustic guitars. The sources provided are focused on general wood characteristics and not specific to the type of guitar. Additionally, the sources do not provide a comprehensive list of all possible wood species used for acoustic guitar construction. Furthermore, the report lacks information on the specific acoustic characteristics of River Oak and the appearance of Cocobolo. The report also does not address the construction processes of acoustic classical guitars.
